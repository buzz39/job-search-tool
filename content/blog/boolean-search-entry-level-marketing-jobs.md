Entry-level marketing job searches can get crowded fast. A search for "marketing assistant" may return internships, commission-heavy sales jobs, unpaid social media gigs, senior growth roles, and vague "brand ambassador" postings that are not a real marketing career path.

Boolean search helps you describe the marketing role you actually want. You can group junior title variations, add channel keywords like SEO or email marketing, and exclude internships, senior roles, and sales-heavy postings. If you want a quick starting point, the [Job Search Query Builder](/) can turn your preferred titles, skills, and location into a reusable search string.

## Why Entry-Level Marketing Searches Are Noisy

Marketing titles vary widely by company size and industry. One employer may post a "marketing coordinator" role, while another uses "digital marketing associate," "content coordinator," "social media specialist," or "growth marketing assistant" for similar early-career work.

Broad marketing searches also pull in roles that may not match your goals:

- unpaid or low-paid internships
- commission-only sales jobs labeled as marketing
- brand ambassador and street-team roles
- senior manager jobs that mention entry-level tasks
- agency roles that require years of client experience
- vague business development jobs with little marketing work

A better Boolean search combines junior marketing titles with channel or skill keywords, then removes repeated poor-fit terms.

## Core Boolean Operators for Marketing Job Searches

Use these building blocks before adapting the examples below.

**Quotes** keep exact phrases together:

```text
"marketing coordinator"
```

**OR** captures title variations:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "marketing assistant" OR "digital marketing associate")
```

**AND** adds required skills or channels:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "marketing assistant") AND (SEO OR email OR social)
```

**NOT** removes irrelevant postings:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "marketing assistant") AND digital NOT internship NOT sales
```

On Google-style searches, use minus signs when `NOT` is ignored:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "marketing assistant") digital -internship -sales
```

## Boolean Strings by Entry-Level Marketing Role

### General Marketing Coordinator Roles

Start with a broad entry-level title group:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "marketing assistant" OR "marketing associate" OR "junior marketer") AND (digital OR content OR campaign OR email OR social) NOT internship NOT intern NOT "commission only"
```

If you only want full-time roles, add employment-type language:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "marketing associate") AND ("full time" OR full-time) AND (digital OR campaign OR content) NOT internship NOT sales
```

### Digital Marketing Jobs

Digital marketing postings may mention tools, channels, or analytics instead of a single title:

```text
("digital marketing coordinator" OR "digital marketing associate" OR "marketing specialist" OR "growth marketing associate") AND (SEO OR PPC OR analytics OR "Google Ads" OR email) NOT senior NOT manager NOT internship
```

For performance marketing entry points, focus on paid media and measurement terms:

```text
("paid media coordinator" OR "performance marketing associate" OR "digital marketing assistant") AND ("Google Ads" OR Meta OR analytics OR campaign) NOT senior NOT "5 years"
```

### Social Media Marketing Roles

Social media job titles are especially inconsistent, so include several variations:

```text
("social media coordinator" OR "social media assistant" OR "social media associate" OR "community coordinator") AND (content OR Instagram OR TikTok OR LinkedIn OR scheduling) NOT internship NOT influencer NOT volunteer
```

If you want business-side social media rather than creator roles, add company or campaign language:

```text
("social media coordinator" OR "community coordinator") AND (brand OR campaign OR analytics OR content calendar) NOT influencer NOT "personal brand" NOT unpaid
```

### Content Marketing and SEO Roles

For writing-heavy marketing jobs, combine content titles with SEO and editorial terms:

```text
("content marketing coordinator" OR "content marketing associate" OR "content writer" OR "SEO assistant") AND (SEO OR blog OR editorial OR copywriting) NOT senior NOT freelance NOT internship
```

If you are open to hybrid content and email roles, try:

```text
("content coordinator" OR "marketing coordinator") AND (email OR newsletter OR blog OR SEO) AND (entry-level OR junior OR associate) NOT manager NOT director
```

### Remote Entry-Level Marketing Jobs

Remote entry-level marketing searches need extra filtering because many postings are hybrid or commission-heavy:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "digital marketing associate" OR "social media coordinator") AND (remote OR "work from home") AND (content OR email OR SEO OR social) NOT hybrid NOT onsite NOT "commission only"
```

For Google, use minus signs:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "digital marketing associate") remote (SEO OR email OR content) -hybrid -onsite -internship -sales
```

## Search Google for Company Career Pages

Google can uncover entry-level marketing roles that are posted on applicant-tracking systems before they spread to large job boards.

For Greenhouse-hosted jobs:

```text
site:greenhouse.io ("marketing coordinator" OR "digital marketing associate") (SEO OR content OR email) -senior -manager -internship
```

For Lever-hosted jobs:

```text
site:jobs.lever.co ("social media coordinator" OR "marketing associate") remote -senior -internship -sales
```

To search broadly across career pages:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "marketing assistant" OR "digital marketing associate") ("careers" OR "jobs") (SEO OR social OR email) -internship -commission
```

## Exclusions That Save Time

Common negative terms for entry-level marketing searches include:

```text
NOT internship NOT intern NOT senior NOT manager NOT director NOT sales NOT "commission only" NOT unpaid NOT volunteer NOT "brand ambassador"
```

Do not add every exclusion at once. If you are looking for an internship, for example, you should not exclude `internship`. Start with the two or three bad-fit terms that show up repeatedly, then refine after reviewing the first page of results.

## Platform Tips

**LinkedIn:** Use a shorter title group in the keyword box, then filter by experience level, date posted, location, and remote status. If sales jobs keep appearing, add `NOT sales NOT "business development"`.

**Indeed:** Indeed often works better with simpler strings. Try one role family at a time, such as content marketing or social media, instead of combining every marketing channel in one query.

**Google:** Google is best for searching company career pages and applicant-tracking systems. Use quotes for exact titles and minus signs for exclusions.

**Startup boards:** Add tool or channel terms like `HubSpot`, `SEO`, `email`, `analytics`, `content`, or `TikTok` so broad operations and sales roles do not dominate the results.

## A Reusable Entry-Level Marketing Template

Use this template as a starting point:

```text
("TITLE 1" OR "TITLE 2" OR "TITLE 3") AND (CHANNEL 1 OR CHANNEL 2 OR SKILL 1) AND (entry-level OR junior OR associate OR coordinator) NOT senior NOT manager NOT internship
```

Example:

```text
("marketing coordinator" OR "digital marketing associate" OR "content coordinator") AND (SEO OR email OR content) AND (entry-level OR junior OR associate) NOT senior NOT sales NOT internship
```

Run the search, scan the first page, and adjust. If results are too broad, add a channel or tool. If results are too narrow, remove one required skill or split the search into separate content, social, and digital marketing searches.

## Final Checklist

Before saving an entry-level marketing search, make sure it includes:

- two to five junior title variations
- one marketing channel or skill group
- exclusions for senior, sales-heavy, or unpaid postings
- remote or location terms if they matter
- a shorter version for job boards that do not handle long strings well

Entry-level marketing roles are easier to find when your search reflects how companies actually write job titles. Group the common title variations, add one clear marketing channel, and remove the repeated noise before creating saved searches or job alerts.

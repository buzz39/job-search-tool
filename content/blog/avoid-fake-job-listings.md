Fake job listings are everywhere. A 2025 FTC report tracked over $490 million in reported losses from job scams in the US alone, and that only counts people who actually filed complaints. Every job board has them — LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, even company career pages get spoofed.

The people behind fake listings are not amateurs. They scrape real job descriptions, clone company branding, register lookalike domains, and build credible-looking application workflows designed to harvest resumes, collect personal documents, or trick applicants into sending money. Here is how to spot them before you waste time — or worse, hand over sensitive information.

## Red Flags That Take Less Than 30 Seconds

Start with the quick checks. If any of these fire, you can probably skip the rest and move on.

**Too good to be true pay.** Entry-level "data entry" roles offering $40/hour fully remote with no experience required are almost never real. If the listed salary is 2x market rate and the requirements are thin, it is a bait listing.

**No company name or a name you cannot verify.** Some legitimate staffing agencies omit the client name, but if the listing hides the hiring company *and* the agency details are missing or vague, walk away. Search the company name plus "scam" or "review" — if nothing comes up, that is its own red flag.

**Communication outside the platform.** A listing that asks you to contact a Gmail address, a WhatsApp number, or a Telegram handle instead of applying through the board's own system is usually a scam. Real recruiters use their corporate email.

**"We will send you a check for equipment."** Any listing that mentions sending you a check to buy equipment, software, or training materials is a scam. The check will bounce after you have spent your own money.

**Interview by text or chat only.** Legitimate companies do not hire after a Telegram or WhatsApp text-only interview. Video or phone calls are standard. Text-only hiring is a strong signal of a fake listing.

## Use Search Operators to Verify the Listing

Boolean search helps you cross-check a suspicious listing quickly. If a posting claims to be from "TechCorp" for a "remote data analyst" role, run these searches:

- `"exact job title" company name scam` — Check if anyone has flagged it.
- `site:company-careers-page.com "job title"` — Does the role actually appear on the official careers page?
- `"too good phrase from listing"` — Scammers copy-paste. Paste a unique sentence from the description in quotes and search.

If the role does not appear on the company's own careers page, call the company directly using a phone number from their official website — not one from the listing — and ask if the role is real.

## Check the Domain and Email

Lookalike domains are a common trick: `company-jobs.com` instead of `company.com`, or `compaany.com` with an extra letter. Hover over links before clicking. Check the sender's email domain if you get a message — legitimate recruiters use `@company.com`, not `@company-careers.org` or `@gmail.com`.

Use `whois` or a domain lookup tool. If the domain was registered two weeks ago and claims to represent a Fortune 500 company, it is fake.

## What They Are Actually After

Understanding the scammer's goal helps you spot patterns:

- **Identity theft.** They want your full name, address, date of birth, and Social Security number — everything needed to open accounts in your name. They collect this through fake "background check" forms.
- **Resume harvesting.** Your resume has your phone, email, work history, and sometimes references. Scammers sell these in bulk to other fraudsters.
- **Upfront payments.** "Pay $200 for training materials and we will reimburse you." You will not be reimbursed.
- **Money laundering.** Some fake listings are fronts for reshipping scams or money mule operations. If the job involves receiving packages and forwarding them, or receiving money and wiring it elsewhere, you are being recruited for a crime.

## Steps to Take If You Spot One

Report it. Every major job board has a "report listing" button. Use it. The faster fake listings get taken down, the fewer people get caught.

If you already applied and shared personal information, freeze your credit with the three major bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion), check your credit report for unfamiliar accounts, and file a report at `reportfraud.ftc.gov`.

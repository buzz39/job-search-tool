HR and recruiting job searches can get messy because the same function appears under many different titles. A recruiter role may be posted as "talent acquisition specialist," "sourcer," "recruiting coordinator," or "technical recruiter." A people operations role may be listed as "HR generalist," "people partner," "employee experience specialist," or "HR operations analyst."

Boolean search helps you search across those title variations without reviewing every broad human resources posting by hand. Instead of searching one title at a time, you can group similar roles, add the tools or industries that matter, and remove postings that are too senior, too junior, or focused on agency sales instead of internal HR work. If you want a shortcut, the [Job Search Query Builder](/) can turn your preferred titles, skills, and location into a reusable search string.

## Why HR and Recruiting Searches Need Better Keywords

Human resources is full of overlapping language. Some companies still use traditional titles like "HR manager" and "HR coordinator." Others use people-team language such as "people operations," "people partner," or "employee success." Recruiting has the same problem: one company posts a "corporate recruiter" role while another posts a "talent acquisition partner" role for similar work.

Broad searches can also pull in unrelated roles:

- staffing agency sales jobs
- recruiter roles that are fully commission based
- senior HR director roles when you want an individual contributor job
- payroll-only jobs when you want broader people operations work
- recruiting coordinator roles when you want full-cycle recruiting
- HR internships when you need a permanent role

A good Boolean string tells the search engine both what to include and what to avoid.

## Core Boolean Operators for HR Job Searches

Use these operators as building blocks before adapting the longer examples below.

**Quotes** keep exact titles together:

```text
"talent acquisition specialist"
```

**OR** captures title variations:

```text
("recruiter" OR "talent acquisition specialist" OR "talent acquisition partner")
```

**AND** adds required context:

```text
("HR generalist" OR "people operations") AND benefits
```

**NOT** removes roles that do not fit:

```text
("corporate recruiter" OR "talent acquisition partner") NOT agency NOT commission
```

On Google-style searches, a minus sign may work better than `NOT`:

```text
("corporate recruiter" OR "talent acquisition partner") -agency -commission
```

## Boolean Strings for Recruiting Roles

### Corporate Recruiter and Talent Acquisition Jobs

Use this string when you want in-house recruiting roles rather than staffing agency positions:

```text
("corporate recruiter" OR "internal recruiter" OR "talent acquisition specialist" OR "talent acquisition partner") AND (hiring OR sourcing OR interviewing) NOT agency NOT staffing NOT commission
```

If you are open to general recruiting titles but want to avoid sales-heavy agency work, add stronger exclusions:

```text
(recruiter OR "talent acquisition") AND ("full cycle" OR sourcing OR interviews) NOT "business development" NOT "account manager" NOT commission NOT agency
```

### Technical Recruiter Roles

Technical recruiting searches work best when you include both title and skill context:

```text
("technical recruiter" OR "tech recruiter" OR "engineering recruiter") AND (software OR engineering OR developers OR SaaS) NOT agency NOT commission
```

For remote technical recruiting jobs, add location flexibility directly:

```text
("technical recruiter" OR "engineering recruiter") AND (remote OR "work from home") AND (software OR startup OR SaaS) NOT agency NOT contract
```

### Recruiting Coordinator Roles

Coordinator postings often overlap with administrative assistant roles. Require recruiting workflow language to keep results relevant:

```text
("recruiting coordinator" OR "talent acquisition coordinator" OR "recruitment coordinator") AND (scheduling OR interviews OR ATS OR onboarding) NOT intern NOT internship
```

If you are early in your career, avoid excluding too much. Instead, include entry-level terms:

```text
("recruiting coordinator" OR "talent acquisition coordinator") AND (entry-level OR junior OR coordinator OR scheduling) NOT unpaid NOT internship
```

### Sourcer Roles

Sourcing jobs may be posted separately from recruiter jobs, especially at larger companies:

```text
("talent sourcer" OR sourcer OR "sourcing specialist") AND (LinkedIn OR Boolean OR outreach OR pipelines) NOT procurement NOT "strategic sourcing"
```

The procurement exclusion matters because "sourcing" is also used in supply chain and vendor management.

## Boolean Strings for HR and People Operations Roles

### HR Generalist Jobs

For broad HR roles, include common responsibility keywords:

```text
("HR generalist" OR "human resources generalist" OR "people operations specialist") AND (benefits OR employee relations OR onboarding OR compliance) NOT intern NOT recruiter
```

If you want startup people operations roles, use modern title variations:

```text
("people operations" OR "people ops" OR "employee experience") AND (onboarding OR benefits OR HRIS OR compliance) AND (startup OR SaaS) NOT recruiter
```

### HR Business Partner Roles

HRBP searches should include both the acronym and the full phrase:

```text
("HR business partner" OR HRBP OR "people business partner") AND (employee relations OR workforce planning OR managers OR performance) NOT recruiter NOT coordinator
```

For senior HRBP roles, add strategic language:

```text
("senior HR business partner" OR "Sr HRBP" OR "people business partner") AND (strategy OR leadership OR workforce planning) NOT director NOT recruiter
```

### HR Operations and HRIS Roles

HR operations roles can overlap with payroll, benefits, systems, and compliance. Use tool names if you have platform experience:

```text
("HR operations" OR "HR ops" OR "HRIS analyst" OR "people operations analyst") AND (Workday OR BambooHR OR Greenhouse OR Lever OR reporting) NOT recruiter
```

For benefits-focused roles:

```text
("benefits specialist" OR "benefits analyst" OR "total rewards specialist") AND (open enrollment OR compensation OR HRIS) NOT sales NOT insurance agent
```

## Search Specific Job Boards and Career Pages

Boolean strings become more useful when you pair them with the right site.

For LinkedIn-style searches:

```text
("talent acquisition partner" OR "corporate recruiter") AND remote NOT agency NOT commission
```

For Google searches across company career pages:

```text
site:boards.greenhouse.io ("people operations" OR "HR generalist") (remote OR hybrid) NOT recruiter
```

For Lever-hosted postings:

```text
site:jobs.lever.co ("technical recruiter" OR "engineering recruiter") (remote OR "United States") NOT agency
```

For Workday career sites, combine title variations with company or industry terms:

```text
site:myworkdayjobs.com ("HR business partner" OR HRBP) (SaaS OR fintech OR healthcare) NOT recruiter
```

## Add Skills, Tools, and Industry Terms

Once your title group is working, add keywords that signal the right environment. HR and recruiting searches often benefit from terms like:

- ATS, Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, iCIMS, Ashby
- HRIS, BambooHR, ADP, UKG, Rippling, payroll
- employee relations, onboarding, benefits, compliance
- sourcing, pipelines, outreach, interviews, offer management
- SaaS, healthcare, fintech, manufacturing, nonprofit, education

For example:

```text
("people operations specialist" OR "HR generalist") AND (BambooHR OR Rippling OR ADP) AND (onboarding OR benefits) NOT recruiter
```

Or for recruiting:

```text
("talent acquisition partner" OR "corporate recruiter") AND (Greenhouse OR Lever OR Ashby) AND (sourcing OR "offer management") NOT agency
```

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Do not search only for "HR". It is too broad and can match unrelated abbreviations, internal acronyms, or postings where HR is mentioned only once.

Do not rely on one modern title either. Searching only for "people operations" may miss companies that still use "human resources generalist" for the same work.

Be careful with exclusions. Removing "recruiter" is useful for HR operations searches, but it can hide relevant people-team roles at small companies where one person owns both HR and recruiting.

Finally, test your query in stages. Start with titles, scan the results, then add tools, industries, and exclusions only after you see what kind of noise appears.


For people operations:

```text
("people operations" OR "HR generalist" OR "employee experience specialist") AND (onboarding OR benefits OR HRIS OR compliance) NOT recruiter
```

For HRBP roles:

```text
("HR business partner" OR HRBP OR "people business partner") AND (employee relations OR workforce planning OR performance) NOT coordinator NOT recruiter
```

For technical recruiting:

```text
("technical recruiter" OR "engineering recruiter") AND (software OR SaaS OR developers) AND (Greenhouse OR Lever OR sourcing) NOT agency
```

The best HR job search string is not the longest one. It is the one that matches how your target employers describe the work. Start with title variations, add the tools and responsibilities you actually want to use, and keep refining until the results page is specific enough to act on.

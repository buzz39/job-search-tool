Operations roles can be surprisingly hard to search for because the word "operations" appears in almost every industry. A broad search might return warehouse supervisor jobs, business operations analyst roles, revenue operations openings, office manager posts, logistics roles, people operations jobs, and senior operations leadership positions all on the same results page.

Boolean search helps you separate the kind of operations work you want from the noise. By grouping title variations, adding industry or function keywords, and excluding unrelated roles, you can make job boards and Google searches return much cleaner results. If you want a quick starting point, the [Job Search Query Builder](/) can turn your preferred titles, skills, and location into a reusable query.

## Why Operations Job Searches Get Messy

Operations is a broad function, and employers use the label in different ways. Depending on the company, an operations role might focus on:

- process improvement and internal workflows
- business operations and analytics
- revenue operations or sales operations
- logistics, supply chain, and fulfillment
- people operations and HR systems
- office operations and administrative coordination
- general management or regional leadership

That variety is useful if you are exploring, but it becomes frustrating when you know what you want. Boolean operators let you describe both sides of the search: the titles and responsibilities you want to include, and the repeated false positives you want to remove.

## Core Boolean Operators for Operations Searches

Use these building blocks before adapting the longer examples below.

**Quotes** keep exact phrases together:

```text
"operations manager"
```

**OR** captures title variations:

```text
("operations manager" OR "operations lead" OR "business operations manager")
```

**AND** adds required context:

```text
("operations manager" OR "operations lead") AND SaaS
```

**NOT** removes irrelevant postings:

```text
("operations manager" OR "business operations manager") AND SaaS NOT warehouse NOT retail
```

On Google-style searches, a minus sign often works better than `NOT`:

```text
("operations manager" OR "business operations manager") SaaS -warehouse -retail
```

## Boolean Strings by Operations Role

### Business Operations Roles

Use this string when you want cross-functional operations work involving planning, analysis, and process improvement:

```text
("business operations manager" OR "business operations analyst" OR "bizops" OR "strategy and operations") AND (process OR analytics OR reporting OR planning) NOT warehouse NOT logistics
```

For tech-company business operations, add SaaS or startup context:

```text
("business operations" OR "strategy and operations" OR "bizops") AND (SaaS OR software OR startup) AND (SQL OR dashboards OR metrics OR process) NOT "data entry"
```

If you are early career, include coordinator and analyst titles:

```text
("operations analyst" OR "business operations analyst" OR "operations coordinator") AND (reporting OR process OR Excel OR SQL) NOT warehouse NOT "call center"
```

### Operations Manager Roles

Operations manager searches need strong exclusions because the title appears in many frontline environments:

```text
("operations manager" OR "operations lead" OR "senior operations manager") AND (process OR planning OR cross-functional OR metrics) NOT warehouse NOT retail NOT restaurant
```

For remote-friendly operations roles, add distributed-work language:

```text
("operations manager" OR "operations lead") AND (remote OR "work from home" OR distributed) AND (SaaS OR software OR startup) NOT warehouse NOT facilities
```

If you want people-management roles, require leadership terms:

```text
("operations manager" OR "senior operations manager") AND (team OR leadership OR coaching OR "direct reports") AND (process OR metrics) NOT "assistant manager"
```

### Revenue Operations and Sales Operations

Revenue operations roles may be posted under RevOps, sales operations, GTM operations, or CRM operations. Start with title coverage:

```text
("revenue operations" OR RevOps OR "sales operations" OR "GTM operations") AND (Salesforce OR CRM OR pipeline OR forecasting) NOT "account executive" NOT recruiter
```

For analyst-level RevOps roles:

```text
("revenue operations analyst" OR "sales operations analyst" OR "CRM analyst") AND (Salesforce OR HubSpot OR reporting OR dashboards) NOT sales NOT "cold calling"
```

For manager-level roles, add ownership terms:

```text
("revenue operations manager" OR "sales operations manager" OR "GTM operations manager") AND (forecasting OR territory OR compensation OR process) AND (SaaS OR B2B)
```

### People Operations Roles

People operations searches often overlap with HR, recruiting, payroll, and office management. Use title variations plus HR systems or employee experience keywords:

```text
("people operations" OR "people ops" OR "HR operations" OR "employee experience") AND (onboarding OR HRIS OR benefits OR payroll) NOT recruiter NOT "talent acquisition"
```

For generalist people operations roles:

```text
("people operations generalist" OR "HR generalist" OR "employee experience specialist") AND (onboarding OR compliance OR benefits OR HRIS) NOT recruiter
```

For remote people ops roles:

```text
("people operations" OR "HR operations") AND (remote OR distributed OR "work from home") AND (HRIS OR onboarding OR benefits) NOT recruiter
```

### Logistics and Supply Chain Operations

If you are targeting physical operations, logistics, or supply chain, include those terms directly instead of relying on the generic word operations:

```text
("logistics coordinator" OR "logistics manager" OR "supply chain analyst" OR "operations coordinator") AND (inventory OR fulfillment OR shipping OR procurement) NOT driver NOT "delivery driver"
```

For supply chain analyst roles:

```text
("supply chain analyst" OR "operations analyst" OR "logistics analyst") AND (forecasting OR inventory OR procurement OR ERP) NOT warehouse
```

For fulfillment leadership roles:

```text
("fulfillment manager" OR "warehouse operations manager" OR "logistics operations manager") AND (inventory OR safety OR scheduling OR process) NOT driver
```

## Platform-Specific Tips

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn can struggle with very long strings. Start with a compact title group, then use filters for location, experience level, remote status, and date posted.

```text
("business operations" OR "strategy and operations" OR "operations analyst") AND SaaS
```

If the results are too broad, add one exclusion at a time instead of pasting a giant negative list.

### Indeed

Indeed often performs better with simpler phrasing. Use exact titles and a few core skills, then rely on filters for job type and salary.

```text
"operations analyst" SQL reporting remote
```

For operations manager roles, run separate searches for corporate operations and frontline operations so you can tune exclusions differently.

### Google

Google is useful for searching company career pages and applicant tracking systems:

```text
("business operations manager" OR "operations analyst") (SaaS OR startup) (remote OR "United States") -warehouse -retail -restaurant
```

To search common ATS pages, run focused searches like:

```text
site:greenhouse.io "business operations" SaaS remote -warehouse
```

```text
site:lever.co "revenue operations" Salesforce remote
```

## Exclusions That Save Time

Common exclusions for corporate operations searches include:

```text
NOT warehouse NOT retail NOT restaurant NOT driver NOT cashier NOT "assistant manager" NOT facilities
```

For logistics or supply chain searches, do not blindly exclude warehouse or fulfillment terms because those may be relevant. Instead, remove the specific false positives you keep seeing, such as driver, delivery driver, or retail associate.

## A Simple Workflow for Better Operations Searches

1. Choose the operations lane you want: business operations, RevOps, people ops, logistics, or general operations management.
2. List three to six title variations for that lane.
3. Add one or two context terms such as SaaS, Salesforce, HRIS, SQL, inventory, or process improvement.
4. Search once, then identify the repeated irrelevant results.
5. Add exclusions only for the noise that actually appears.
6. Save the best query and reuse it for weekly job alerts.

A good operations Boolean search is not about adding every possible keyword. It is about naming the right operations function, including the tools or responsibilities that prove fit, and excluding the unrelated roles that flood broad job board searches.

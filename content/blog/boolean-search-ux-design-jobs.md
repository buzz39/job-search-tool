UX and product design job searches can become noisy because employers use overlapping titles for very different work. A search for "designer" may return graphic design, brand design, web production, CAD, instructional design, and product design roles on the same page. Even within digital product teams, postings may use "UX designer," "product designer," "interaction designer," "UI designer," "UX researcher," or "content designer" for similar responsibilities.

Boolean search helps you describe the exact design lane you want. By grouping title variations, adding product or research keywords, and excluding unrelated design work, you can make LinkedIn, Indeed, Google, and company career pages return much cleaner results. If you want a quick starting point, the [Job Search Query Builder](/) can turn your target titles, skills, and location into a reusable search string.

## Why UX and Product Design Searches Need Better Filters

Design hiring language is inconsistent. One company might call a role "product designer" when it includes research, prototyping, visual design, and design systems. Another might split the same work across separate UX, UI, and research roles. Broad searches often include:

- graphic designer and brand designer jobs
- front-end developer roles that mention UI
- web designer or WordPress production roles
- industrial, CAD, or architectural design jobs
- senior design leadership roles when you want individual contributor work
- UX researcher roles when you are looking for hands-on product design

Boolean operators let you capture the titles you do want while excluding repeated false positives.

## Core Boolean Operators for Design Job Searches

Use these building blocks before adapting the examples below.

**Quotes** keep exact phrases together:

```text
"product designer"
```

**OR** captures title variations:

```text
("product designer" OR "UX designer" OR "user experience designer")
```

**AND** adds required context:

```text
("product designer" OR "UX designer") AND (SaaS OR mobile OR "design system")
```

**NOT** removes unrelated postings:

```text
("product designer" OR "UX designer") NOT graphic NOT CAD NOT architect
```

On Google-style searches, use minus signs for exclusions:

```text
("product designer" OR "UX designer") -graphic -CAD -architect -"visual merchandiser"
```

## Boolean Strings by UX and Product Design Role

### Product Designer Roles

Use this string when you want end-to-end product design work involving discovery, flows, prototyping, and UI execution:

```text
("product designer" OR "senior product designer" OR "digital product designer") AND (UX OR "user experience" OR prototype OR Figma) NOT graphic NOT brand NOT CAD
```

For SaaS product design roles, add software and workflow language:

```text
("product designer" OR "UX designer") AND (SaaS OR software OR platform OR B2B) AND (Figma OR prototype OR "user flows" OR "design system") NOT marketing NOT print
```

If you are earlier in your design career, include associate and junior titles without opening the search too widely:

```text
("associate product designer" OR "junior product designer" OR "UX designer I" OR "product design intern") AND (portfolio OR Figma OR prototype) NOT graphic NOT CAD
```

### UX Designer and Interaction Designer Roles

UX designer searches should include interaction design and information architecture terms, especially when you want roles focused on flows and usability:

```text
("UX designer" OR "user experience designer" OR "interaction designer") AND (wireframes OR prototypes OR usability OR "information architecture") NOT graphic NOT "web developer"
```

For mobile UX roles:

```text
("UX designer" OR "product designer") AND (mobile OR iOS OR Android OR app) AND (prototype OR flows OR usability) NOT game NOT graphic
```

For enterprise or B2B UX work:

```text
("UX designer" OR "interaction designer" OR "product designer") AND (enterprise OR B2B OR SaaS) AND (workflows OR dashboards OR "complex systems") NOT marketing
```

### UI Designer and Visual Product Design Roles

If you want interface design but not print or brand-only roles, require product context:

```text
("UI designer" OR "visual product designer" OR "interface designer") AND (app OR SaaS OR product OR Figma) NOT print NOT packaging NOT "graphic designer"
```

For roles that combine UI and design systems:

```text
("UI designer" OR "product designer") AND ("design system" OR components OR tokens OR accessibility) AND (Figma OR Storybook) NOT graphic
```

### UX Research Roles

UX research postings may be listed under UXR, user researcher, design researcher, or product researcher. Start with title coverage:

```text
("UX researcher" OR "user researcher" OR UXR OR "design researcher" OR "product researcher") AND (interviews OR usability OR surveys OR qualitative) NOT recruiter NOT market
```

For mixed-methods research roles:

```text
("UX researcher" OR "user researcher") AND ("mixed methods" OR qualitative OR quantitative OR surveys OR analytics) AND (SaaS OR product)
```

For contract UX research searches:

```text
("UX researcher" OR "user researcher") AND (contract OR freelance OR consultant) AND (usability OR interviews OR synthesis) NOT recruiter
```

### Content Design and UX Writing Roles

Content design titles vary widely. Use title synonyms plus product-writing terms:

```text
("content designer" OR "UX writer" OR "product writer" OR "content strategist") AND (microcopy OR "user flows" OR product OR Figma) NOT marketing NOT "social media"
```

For senior content design roles:

```text
("senior content designer" OR "lead content designer" OR "principal UX writer") AND (strategy OR systems OR taxonomy OR product) NOT marketing
```

## Remote UX and Product Design Search Strings

Remote design searches work best when you combine remote language with product-specific keywords:

```text
("product designer" OR "UX designer") AND (remote OR "work from home" OR distributed) AND (Figma OR prototype OR SaaS) NOT graphic NOT CAD
```

For remote UX research:

```text
("UX researcher" OR "user researcher") AND (remote OR distributed) AND (interviews OR usability OR research) NOT recruiter
```

For Google searches across applicant tracking systems, try:

```text
site:greenhouse.io ("product designer" OR "UX designer") remote Figma -graphic -CAD
```

```text
site:lever.co ("UX researcher" OR "user researcher") remote usability -recruiter
```

## Platform-Specific Tips

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn can be sensitive to very long strings. Start with a compact title group and use filters for location, workplace type, experience level, and date posted.

```text
("product designer" OR "UX designer") AND Figma
```

If results are too broad, add one exclusion at a time. Avoid pasting a huge negative list until you know which false positives are actually appearing.

### Indeed

Indeed often works better with simpler phrasing:

```text
"product designer" Figma remote
```

Run separate searches for product design, UX research, and content design instead of trying to cover every design role in one query.

### Google

Google is useful for finding postings on company career pages and applicant tracking systems:

```text
("product designer" OR "UX designer") (remote OR "United States") (Figma OR prototype) -graphic -CAD -print
```

To search company career pages directly:

```text
site:jobs.ashbyhq.com "product designer" Figma remote
```

```text
site:boards.greenhouse.io "content designer" product remote
```

## Exclusions That Save Time

Common exclusions for digital product design searches include:

```text
NOT graphic NOT brand NOT print NOT packaging NOT CAD NOT architect NOT "visual merchandiser" NOT "web developer"
```

Do not exclude every adjacent field automatically. If you are open to brand systems, marketing design, or front-end prototyping roles, keep those terms available and remove only the noise that repeatedly appears.

## A Simple Workflow for Better Design Searches

1. Choose the lane you want: product design, UX design, UI design, UX research, content design, or design systems.
2. List three to six title variations for that lane.
3. Add two or three required signals such as Figma, prototyping, usability, SaaS, mobile, accessibility, or design systems.
4. Run the search once and scan the first page for irrelevant patterns.
5. Add exclusions only for the false positives you actually see.
6. Save the best query and reuse it weekly as a job alert.

A strong UX job search is not about writing the longest Boolean string possible. It is about making each query specific enough to match the way hiring teams describe the role you want. Start focused, test the results, and keep the search strings that consistently surface relevant design jobs.
